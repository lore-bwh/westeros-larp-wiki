World Overview

A realm of crowns, oaths, and blood.

The Nature of the Realm

Westeros is not a peaceful land of song and ceremony. It is a realm of inheritance, violence, ambition, and consequence. Great Houses rise and fall on the strength of their bloodlines, their holdings, their loyalties, and the fury of their enemies. The Realm is shaped by canon, recorded history, and the consequences of every action taken within it.

In Blood Without Honor, players are expected to understand the foundations of the Realm before altering its course. To act without understanding the weight of history is not bravery—it is recklessness. Great power demands greater responsibility, and every House must live with the story it writes.

Canon & Consequence

A canon-based action must be supported by established lore, precedent, or recognized House tradition. It must be carried out IC through proper channels and remain consistent with the Realm's systems, titles, and limitations.

Immersion Matters

Players are expected to remain in character whenever possible. OOC coordination may not be used to bypass IC limitations or distort the setting. A character can only act on what they could reasonably know, understand, and justify within the world.

The Law of the Realm

Rules that shape history.

I

Canon & Recorded History

The Realm is defined by what has been established through recognized lore, accepted history, and the rules of the setting. What is written becomes part of the world. To erase it lightly is to insult the memory of the dead.

II

Character Realism

Characters must remain grounded in lineage, station, culture, and circumstance. Extraordinary traits, bloodlines, or abilities require proper justification. A House does not become a dragon simply because a player wishes it to be so.

III

Metagaming is Forbidden

A character may act on information only if it is reasonable for that character to know. A raven, decree, messenger, or direct IC communication may inform action. OOC knowledge does not rewrite what the character could possibly know.

IV

Conflict Requires Cause

Wars, coups, and political confrontations require meaningful cause. The Realm does not accept war declared simply to indulge a player’s desire for battle. Legitimate cause matters: blood, fealty, succession, land, honor, or a lawful grievance.

V

Events Make History

Lore events drive the progression of the Realm. Battles, weddings, coronations, betrayals, sieges, and treaties are not decorations. If they are properly conducted and recognized, they may become part of the official record.

VI

Blood & House Limits

Dynasty caps, bloodline control, House limitations, and noble succession matter. A House cannot creatively inflate its own power by ignoring the mechanics meant to preserve the balance of the Realm.

VII

Injury, Captivity & Death

Combat has consequence. Severe injuries can impair military capacity, inheritance, authority, and freedom. Death is permanent unless an approved lore mechanic specifically permits otherwise.

VIII

Ravens, Decrees & Oaths

Ravens are the recognized means of long-distance communication. Properly delivered and acknowledged decrees bind Houses and can shape the political structure of the Realm. Oaths, whether sworn in blood or by noble word, carry weight.

IX

History Cannot Be Rewritten for Shame

Retcons exist to correct genuine errors, not to erase the consequences of loss or poor planning. History is not disposable. It is the memory of the Realm, and it must be respected.

Lore Department

Where continuity is guarded and history is protected.

Guardians of Continuity

The Lore Department exists to preserve the integrity of the setting. It tracks approved events, interprets disputes, and ensures that lore remains consistent with the history already established.

Judgement and Stability

When the world is uncertain, the Lore Department weighs the facts: precedence, the rules of the Realm, House standing, political legitimacy, and historical continuity. In a realm of blood and crown, stability matters as much as spectacle.

History as a Living Thing

The Realm evolves through wars, funerals, marriages, betrayals, and declarations. The Lore Department ensures that these events are documented, measured, and preserved so that the world continues to feel honest and lived in.

Magic & Dragons

Power exists — but it never comes freely.

Dragons and magic are part of the world, but they are not casual tools for personal gain. Extraordinary bloodlines, prophecy, magical inheritance, and dragon claims require proper approval before they are acknowledged as canon. This is not simply flavor. It is a matter of balance, continuity, and the integrity of the realm.

Any character or House seeking to claim dragon lineage, supernatural power, cursed insight, or a rare magical advantage must justify the claim in a manner that matches the setting. If the request threatens the balance of the Realm, destabilizes House politics, or contradicts established history, it may be restricted or denied outright.

Authority Structure

The Lore Department, the Administration, and the final word of the Realm.

Lore Department

The Lore Department is responsible for interpreting events, reviewing legitimacy, ensuring continuity, and preserving the historical memory of the Realm. It governs the standards by which great actions are recognized and how the world remains coherent over time.

Administration

The Administration is the highest authority in the setting. It may confirm, reject, amend, or define rulings where needed, especially in matters of extraordinary power, final disputes, and exceptional policy boundaries. In all matters, the Administration has the final word.

FAQs

Common questions from the Realm.

Do dragons and magic exist?

Yes. They exist in the setting, but their use and claims remain subject to approval and reasonable continuity.

What makes an action canon?

An action becomes canon when it is grounded in lore, recognized by the processes of the Realm, and reflected in the official record.

Can I act on information my character wouldn't know?

No. Metagaming is forbidden. Your character only acts on what they could reasonably know through decrees, ravens, messengers, or direct IC contact.

Who resolves disputes?

The Lore Department handles interpretation and continuity. The Administration has final authority in all matters.

Can a war be declared for no reason?

No. Conflict must have cause, political meaning, and a reasonable connection to the setting. Wars without weight are chaos, not destiny.

Does marriage combine Houses or bloodlines?

No. Marriage creates political ties and may create obligations, but it does not erase identity, leadership, or House limits.

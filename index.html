World Overview

A realm of crowns, oaths, and blood.

The Nature of the Realm

Westeros is not a fairytale kingdom of quiet courtiers and noble songs. It is a realm of weight, inheritance, and violence. Every House carries the burden of its past, every oath has teeth, and every victory can become a source of future ruin. The Realm is shaped by established canon, recorded history, and the consequences of action.

The past is not a decoration. It is the law beneath the law. In Blood Without Honor, the world remembers. The dead remain in memory, the old titles remain in force, and the realm moves forward only because someone chose to write its next chapter with steel, blood, and ambition.

Canon & Consequence

A canon-based action must be supported by lore, precedent, House tradition, and a clear reason for its existence. The Realm does not reward empty spectacle. It rewards legitimacy, pressure, and consequence.

Immersion Matters

The realm only feels alive when players remain in character and act within their limits. OOC claims cannot rewrite IC truth. A character cannot simply do what the player wishes if the character would not know, understand, or be capable of doing so.

The Law of the Realm

Rules that shape history.

I

Canon & Recorded History

The Realm is defined by what has been established through recognized lore, accepted history, and the rules of the setting. All major action must respect this continuity.

II

Character Realism

Characters are shaped by their background, station, blood, culture, and circumstance. Power cannot be claimed casually; extraordinary traits require proper justification and approval.

III

Metagaming is Forbidden

A character can act on information only if it is reasonable for that character to know. The world is not a board to be strategically manipulated through hidden external knowledge.

IV

Conflict Requires Cause

Wars and major political actions need meaningful reason. War without purpose is not honor, it is chaos. The Realm remembers the weak and the cruel alike.

V

Events Make History

Lore events shape the realm. If a battle, wedding, assassination, decree, or succession crisis is properly conducted and recognized, it may become part of the official historical record.

VI

Blood & House Limits

Dynasties, bloodlines, and House caps matter. Every House has a structure, and no leader may bypass those structures by creating unofficial ranks or principals in secret.

VII

Injury, Captivity, and Death

Combat must have consequence. Severe injuries can alter military capacity, inheritance, political standing, and personal freedom. Death is final unless specifically approved by the setting's legitimate mechanics.

VIII

Ravens, Decrees, and Oaths

A raven is not a message you ignore. It is an instrument of legitimacy. Properly delivered and acknowledged decrees can bind houses, claims, and political consequences.

IX

History Cannot Be Rewritten for Shame

Retcons exist to correct genuine errors, not to erase consequences because a player regrets a loss. The realm is not a story that bends to private feelings. It remembers.

Lore Department

Where continuity is guarded and history is protected.

Identity of the Realm

The Lore Department exists to preserve the integrity of the setting. It records approved histories, determines whether a conflict or event is legitimate, and ensures that story beats remain coherent and fair.

Disputes and Judgement

Where there is ambiguity, the Lore Department reviews the circumstances against the rules, canon, and precedent. Major events, politics, inheritance, and extraordinary claims all fall under this authority.

History as a Living Thing

The realm is not static. It evolves through war, funerals, marriage, birth, ambition, and failure. The Lore Department ensures that history is not simply invented overnight, but carefully woven into the continuity of the world.

Magic & Dragons

Power exists — but it never comes freely.

Dragons and magic are part of the world, but they are not casual tools for personal gain. Extraordinary bloodlines, visions, magical inheritance, and dragon claims require proper approval before they are acknowledged as canon. This is not merely a matter of flavor; it is a matter of balance and continuity. The Realm must remain coherent.

Any character or House seeking to claim dragon lineage, magical power, prophetic insight, or an exceptional supernatural connection must justify the claim in a manner that matches the setting. If the request threatens balance or destabilizes the established world, it may be restricted, denied, or rewritten under proper authority.

Authority Structure

The Lore Department and the final word of the Administration.

Lore Department

The Lore Department is responsible for interpreting events, ruling on legitimacy, preserving the continuity of the world, and ensuring that every major action remains consistent with the known rules of the Realm. It is the keeper of the memory of Westeros.

Administration

The Administration is the highest authority in the setting. It may confirm, reject, amend, or define all rulings where required, especially in matters of extraordinary power, policy, final disputes, and necessary correction. In all matters, the Administration has the final word.

FAQs

Common questions from the Realm.

Do dragons and magic exist?

Yes. They exist in the setting, but their use and claims remain subject to approval and reasonable continuity.

What makes a House canon?

Established lore, accepted history, recognized political standing, and proper House continuity all contribute to canon. Common history matters.

Can I act on information I learned outside my character's knowledge?

No. Metagaming is forbidden. Your character only acts on what they could reasonably know through deeds, ravens, messengers, or direct IC contact.

Who resolves disputes?

The Lore Department handles interpretation and continuity. The Administration has final authority in all matters.

Can death be undone?

Death is permanent unless an approved lore mechanic specifically permits a different outcome. The Realm remembers the fallen.

Does a marriage merge Houses?

No. Marriage binds loyalties and may create political ties, but it does not dissolve identity, leadership, or dynastic limits.

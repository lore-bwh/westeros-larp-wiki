World Overview

A realm of crowns, oaths, and blood.

The Nature of the Realm

The Realm is shaped by canon, recorded history, and the consequences of every act committed within it. Every House, family, and claimant contributes to the tide of events that move Westeros forward. What is recorded is not trivial; it is history.

In Blood Without Honor, lore is not mere decoration. It is the framework through which politics, warfare, succession, and consequence are judged. Players are expected to understand the foundations of the Realm before attempting to reshape it.

Canon & Consequence

Actions have weight. A House may rise, fall, be broken, or be remembered. A blade struck in war, a decree issued in winter, a marriage made for influence—these all become part of the story.

Immersion Matters

Players are expected to remain in character and pursue goals with reason, rank, and circumstance in mind. OOC knowledge cannot justify IC action where the character would not have known it.

Lore Guidelines

Rules that shape the history of the Realm.

Canon First

Every action must be grounded in established lore, precedent, recognized tradition, and the structures of the Realm. A House cannot act as if history never existed.

Metagaming is Forbidden

A character may only act on information they could reasonably know through decrees, ravens, messengers, or direct IC contact. If the message was not sent to them, it is not theirs to act upon.

Conflict Has Limits

Wars and political action require cause, structure, and fairness. No House may simply declare war without reason, standing, or strategic legitimacy. The Realm is built on consequence.

Deaths and Injuries Endure

Severe injury can cripple a character's capacity, inheritance, and authority. Death is permanent unless a specific approved lore mechanic permits otherwise.

Houses Matter

House limitations, dynasty caps, succession law, and bloodline status all shape what a House may do in war, politics, and inheritance. Identity, blood, and law are all part of the same chain.

History is Final

Properly recognized events become canon. Retcons are not a tool for regret; they are a last resort for correcting genuine errors or voided records. The Realm remembers.

Magic & Dragons

Power exists — but it is never free.

Dragons and magic are part of the world, but they are not casual tools for personal gain. Extraordinary power, bloodlines, visions, or magical influence must be justified, documented, and approved by the proper authority before it can be considered canon. The Realm does not tolerate unchecked mythmaking.

If a character claims dragon blood, magical insight, supernatural connection, or unusual magical inheritance, it must be supported by a legitimate path within the setting. The Administration may deny, restrict, or modify such claims where they would destabilize the balance of the Realm.

Authority Structure

The Lore Department and the final word of the Administration.

Lore Department

The Lore Department is responsible for the interpretation of events, rule applications, historical continuity, and the enforcement of setting integrity. It records actions, validates roleplay outcomes, and guides disputes in keeping with the established lore of the Realm.

Administration

The Administration is the highest authority in the setting. It may confirm, overturn, or define all interpretations where needed, especially in matters of boundaries, policy, extraordinary powers, and final disputes. In all matters, the Administration has the final word.

FAQs

Common questions from the Realm.

Do dragons and magic exist in Blood Without Honor?

Yes. They exist within the setting, but they require valid in-world justification and approval before they can be treated as canonical.

What is the difference between canon and OOC influence?

Canon is anything established through the lore, history, rules, and recognized IC events of the Realm. OOC coordination or the use of information a character could not reasonably know does not automatically become canon.

Who decides disputes or rule conflicts?

The Lore Department handles interpretation and management of lore disputes, while the Administration ultimately has final authority on all matters.

Can a House just declare war for no reason?

No. Conflict must have cause, political meaning, and a reasonable relationship to the setting. Unjustified or abusive wars are not accepted.

What happens when a character dies?

Death is permanent unless a specific approved lore mechanic permits otherwise. A death can have major consequences for succession, legacy, House stability, and surrounding politics.
